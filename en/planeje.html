The basics before you cross

Gigóia Island sits in Barra da Tijuca and is reachable only by boat. The simplest way is the metro to Jardim Oceânico station: take exit A, walk about two minutes to the pier on Avenida Armando Lombardi and cross on a small ferry. If you drive, park at the shopping centre next to the pier.

The crossing costs a few reais, takes about two minutes and runs 24 hours a day. There is no last boat, which changes the planning: you can stay until late without watching the clock.

No cars circulate on the island. Everything happens on foot along the lanes, or by boat between one island and the next. That is why it is called the Venice of Rio — and why comfortable shoes matter more than anything else in your bag.

What to know before going

🚇 Getting there

Metro to Jardim Oceânico, exit A, then two minutes on foot to the pier on Avenida Armando Lombardi. By car, park at the shopping centre next door. By Uber, ask to be dropped at the pier.

Crossing: about 2 minutes, 24 hours

💰 What a day costs

The return crossing costs a few reais. Add lunch, between R$ 60 and R$ 120 per person at most restaurants, and a boat tour from R$ 50.

Estimate: around R$ 150 per person for a full day

📅 Best time to go

Summer is busier and hotter; the Rio winter is mild and the island empties out. In any month, weekdays are quieter and weekends bring live music and the craft fair.

Tip: late afternoon is the best hour of the day

🎒 What to bring

Sunscreen, insect repellent (the mangroves have mosquitoes late in the afternoon), water, a cap and comfortable shoes. If you plan to get in the water, swimwear and a towel.

Important: cash or Pix — there is no ATM

🚫 What the island doesn't have

No cars, no ATM, no pharmacy. There are two small markets for the basics; pharmacy and supermarket are on the mainland, a few minutes away by boat.

Plan ahead: bring any regular medication

👨‍👩‍👧 With children and pets

With no cars, the island is calm for children, and several places accept small pets. Since almost everything is by the water, agree on rules near the piers.

Check: life jackets in the right size before a tour

Putting the visit together

With half a day, lunch by the lagoon plus a short boat tour is enough. With a full day, you can add breakfast, a water sport and finish with live music. And if you sleep on the island, the tour gets the best slot of all: early morning, when the lagoon is glassy and almost nobody is about.

Book ahead if your visit falls on a weekend, holiday or in high season. Waterfront restaurants fill at lunch, and the island's few places to stay sell out weeks ahead in summer and at New Year.

On safety in the water: ask for a life jacket on any tour and check the boat belongs to a registered operator with a licensed skipper. Open-sea trips are cancelled when the sea is rough — the lagoon routes still run, because the water there is sheltered.

Finally, an honest expectation: the island is not a park with attractions, it is a neighbourhood on the water. Anyone arriving expecting organised entertainment leaves disappointed; anyone arriving to eat well, walk slowly and watch the sunset leaves charmed.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a day on Gigóia Island cost?

The return crossing costs a few reais. With lunch at a lagoon-side restaurant, between R$ 60 and R$ 120 per person, and a boat tour from R$ 50, a full day comes to around R$ 150 per person.

How do I get there by metro?

Get off at Jardim Oceânico station, take exit A and walk about two minutes to the pier on Avenida Armando Lombardi. Boats to the island leave from there every few minutes, 24 hours a day.

Do I need to bring cash?

It is worth it. Restaurants take cards and Pix, but there is no ATM on the island, and the crossing is usually paid in cash or Pix directly to the boatman.

What is the best time of year?

Any of them. Summer is hotter and busier, the Rio winter is mild and the island is empty. What changes the experience most is the day of the week: quiet midweek, music and movement at weekends.

Is there a pharmacy on the island?

No. There are two small markets for day-to-day basics; pharmacy and supermarket are on the mainland, a few minutes away by boat. Bring any regular medication with you.

Is it safe?

It is a residential neighbourhood with residents and normal daily life. The care needed is the same as anywhere by the water: watch children near the piers, wear a life jacket on tours, and use common sense at night in the darker lanes.
