What eating on Gigóia Island is like

Food is the main reason most visitors cross over to the island. Tables sit right at the water's edge, nearly all of them facing the lagoon, and the menu revolves around seafood — shrimp, moqueca stew, fresh fish and sharing plates as the sun goes down.

There is no street: you arrive by boat and walk the lanes to the door. Several restaurants have their own pier, and the boatman will drop you right there if you give him the name of the place. That changes how you plan the day — lunch at one spot, coffee at another, the evening at a third, all on foot or a two-minute crossing away.

The price range is wide: from R$ 20 to R$ 40 per person at a café to R$ 200 or more at a fine-dining house. Most seafood restaurants sit between R$ 60 and R$ 180 per person, with portions that usually serve two.

Where to eat, by style

🦐 Seafood by the lagoon

The island's signature. Cais Bar, Camarão da Barra, Deck Bar, Maracujá da Ilha and Salomé al Mare serve breaded shrimp, moqueca, catch of the day and ceviche, with tables right on the water.

Range: R$ 60 to R$ 200 per person

🍽️ Fine dining

For a special night: Ocyá, contemporary cooking with a reputation beyond the island, and Venne, Mediterranean. These are the priciest houses and the ones that most need a booking.

Range: Venne R$ 80–160 · Ocyá from R$ 200

🍻 Bars and sharing plates

Barefoot-bar atmosphere: Bar Caiçaras, Briza da Gigóia, Boteco da Capivara, Bar do Kai, Bar do Elson and AK Bar. Cold beer, snacks, and at weekends almost always a band playing.

Range: R$ 40 to R$ 200 per person

☕ Cafés and quick bites

To start the day or take a break: Café da Poesia and Cantinho do Café, with specialty coffee, cakes and brunch. For something fast, Parada Burger and Ilha Gourmet, with pizza and sandwiches.

Range: cafés R$ 20–40 · snacks R$ 40–60

🎶 With live music

At weekends, Briza and Caiçaras usually have a band in the afternoon and evening, and other houses follow suit. It is what turns lunch into an all-day plan.

When: mainly Saturday and Sunday

🚤 Arriving by boat

Several places have their own pier and welcome guests straight off the water. Just give the restaurant's name to the boatman at the main pier — they know them all.

Crossing: about 2 minutes

How to choose and book

On weekdays the island is quiet and booking is rarely necessary. Saturdays, Sundays and holidays are another story: the waterfront houses fill up at lunch and the wait for a table can pass an hour. Booking a day or two ahead solves it, and Gigi does that through the chat — you never have to call the restaurant.

If the plan is a front-row table at sunset, arrive about an hour and a half before the sun goes down. That is the most contested slot of the day, and also when the photos come out best.

On what to order: breaded shrimp is on nearly every menu and is the dish most associated with the island. Moqueca, fried fish with pirão and ceviche with cassava chips follow close behind. Portions are generous and usually serve two — worth checking before ordering one each.

Almost every house takes cards and Pix, but there is no ATM on the island. If you are counting on cash for the crossing or a tip, bring it with you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to book a table?

On weekdays, almost never. On weekends and holidays, yes — the lagoon-side houses fill at lunch and late afternoon. Gigi books it through the chat, so you never have to call.

How much does eating on Gigóia Island cost?

A café or snack runs R$ 20 to R$ 40 per person. Bars sit between R$ 40 and R$ 200, and seafood restaurants between R$ 60 and R$ 200, with portions that usually serve two. The island's priciest house starts at R$ 200 per person.

Can I arrive by boat right at the restaurant?

At several of them, yes — they have their own pier. At the main pier, give the boatman the restaurant's name: they know every address by the name of the place.

What is the island's signature dish?

Breaded shrimp is the most ordered and appears on nearly every menu. Moqueca, fried fish with pirão and ceviche with cassava chips complete the classics.

Do restaurants take cards?

Practically all of them take cards and Pix. The catch is that there is no ATM on the island, so bring cash from the mainland if you will need it.

Is there anything for people who don't eat seafood?

Yes. The bars serve sharing plates and Brazilian dishes, Venne is Mediterranean, and there are burgers, pizza and sandwiches. Cafés cover the start of the day.
