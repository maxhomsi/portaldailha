How boat tours work on Gigóia Island

Gigóia Island sits inside the Barra da Tijuca lagoon system — a chain of lagoons and canals linking Lagoa da Tijuca to Lagoa de Marapendi and out to the sea. That is where the island's two nicknames come from: the Venice of Rio, for the lanes and the boats that replace cars, and the Carioca Pantanal, for the wildlife living in the mangroves next door.

First-time visitors often mix up two things. The crossing is transport: the boat that takes you from the mainland to the island in about two minutes, costs a few reais and runs all day. The tour is the guided route that leaves from the island or the main pier and runs through the canals, the mangroves and the neighbouring lagoons.

Most routes leave from the main pier on Avenida Armando Lombardi, near number 370, a few minutes from Jardim Oceânico metro station (exit A). Trips to the Tijucas Islands usually leave from Praia dos Amores, since they head into open sea.

The main routes

🐊 Carioca Pantanal safari

The tour most visitors take. The boat enters the canals and the mangroves, where broad-snouted caimans, capybaras, herons and cormorants are commonly seen. Pedra da Gávea and the Barra cable-stayed bridge appear along the way.

Duration: 45 minutes to 1 hour · Reference: from R$ 50 per person

🌿 Marapendi Reserve

The longer route: the boat leaves Lagoa da Tijuca and enters the Marapendi Canal, which connects one lagoon to the other. It is the best preserved stretch of the system, with mangroves, herons and almost no traffic on weekdays.

Duration: around 2 hours · Reference: on request, varies with group size

🏝️ Tijucas Islands

An archipelago off Barra, with clear water and swimming stops. Packages usually include the return boat, a snorkelling mask, a stand-up paddleboard and water. It depends on the tide and sea conditions, and is cancelled on rough days.

Duration: 3 to 5 hours · Reference: from R$ 150 per person

🌅 Sunset and night tours

Late-afternoon departures to watch the sun set over the lagoon with the silhouette of Pedra da Gávea behind it. Some operators also run a night route, with Barra lit up.

Duration: around 1 hour · Reference: on request

⚡ Jet ski, kayak and stand-up

For those who want to steer. Jet skis, kayaks and paddleboards are rented on Gigóia itself and on neighbouring Ilha da Coroa, always with guidance from the operator.

Duration: per hour · Reference: on request

🏖️ Quebra-Mar and Praia da Reserva

A route that crosses the canals to the sea outlet at Quebra-Mar and continues towards Praia da Reserva. This is the one with the most open scenery.

Duration: 2 to 3 hours · Reference: on request

When to go and what to bring

The island is much quieter on weekdays, and it is easier to book a tour on the spot. On weekends and holidays, book a day or two ahead — the boats fill up and the wait grows.

For wildlife, mornings are best: the caimans tend to sit still on the bank in the sun and the wind is still light, which keeps the water flat for photos. For sunset, leave between an hour and an hour and a half before the sun goes down that day.

Bring sunscreen, insect repellent (the mangroves have mosquitoes in the late afternoon), water, a cap and a light shirt. There is no ATM on the island, so carry cash or have Pix ready. If you are heading to the Tijucas Islands, add swimwear, a towel and a waterproof pouch for your phone.

Safety and the rules of the tour

Gigóia is an easy-going destination, but the tour takes place on water and inside a protected environmental area. It is worth knowing what to expect from an operator and what the law requires.

Before you board

  • A life jacket for everyone on board — in the right size when children are with you.
  • A boat registered with the Port Authority (Capitania dos Portos) and a licensed skipper.
  • Capacity respected. An overloaded boat is a reason not to board.
  • Departures cancelled or rescheduled when the sea is rough or the weather turns.

Passenger boats working the lagoons and canals of Barra follow the NORMAM, the rules of the Brazilian Navy’s Directorate of Ports and Coasts, and are inspected by the Rio de Janeiro Port Authority.

Sharing the place with wildlife

  • The lagoon system lies inside the Marapendi Environmental Protection Area (APA).
  • Broad-snouted caimans, capybaras and herons are wild animals: watch from a distance, never feed them, do not make noise to draw their attention and never try to touch them.
  • Chasing, harassing or feeding wildlife is a crime under Brazilian Federal Law 9,605/1998.
  • No litter in the water — whatever comes on the boat leaves with you.

The operators on the island know these rules. If something about the boarding does not look right, do not board, and talk to us.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a boat tour on Gigóia Island cost?

The Carioca Pantanal safari starts at R$ 50 per person and lasts about 45 minutes. Trips to the Tijucas Islands start at R$ 150 per person and last 3 to 5 hours. These are reference prices from local operators and change through the year.

What is the difference between the crossing and the tour?

The crossing is transport between the mainland and the island: it costs a few reais, takes about two minutes and runs all day. The tour is the guided route through canals, mangroves and lagoons, lasting from 45 minutes to several hours.

Can you really see caimans?

Yes. The lagoon system is home to broad-snouted caimans, and sightings are common on the mangrove route, especially in the morning. They rest on the bank and the boat keeps its distance — never feed or approach the animals.

Do I need to book in advance?

On weekdays you can usually book on the spot. On weekends, holidays and in high season, book ahead. For the Tijucas Islands booking is always required, because departure depends on the tide and the forecast.

Where do I catch the boat?

The main pier is on Avenida Armando Lombardi, near number 370, a few minutes from Jardim Oceânico metro station (exit A). No bridge or road reaches the island: drivers park at the shopping centre next door and cover the last stretch by boat.

Is the tour good for children?

It is. The lagoon route is calm and short, and children enjoy the wildlife. Check that there are life jackets in the right size before boarding, and prefer morning departures, when the sun is gentler.
